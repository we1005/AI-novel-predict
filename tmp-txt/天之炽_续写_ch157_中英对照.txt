【中文版】

第157章 黑匣子

礼车驶出翡冷翠城时，最后一缕月光也被乌云吞了下去。郊外的夜路两旁是连绵的橄榄林，叶片在风里翻涌，发出干燥的沙沙声，像一群人在低低地说话。

瓦莲京娜把车开得很快，但又稳得出奇。她偶尔从后视镜里看一眼车里的少年们。金色的长发被风扯成一面旗，那股忍冬香气混在夜风里，淡淡地缠在每个人的鼻尖。

车是煤与压缩蒸汽驱动的重家伙，散着烧焦石墨和热油的味道。她驾驭它的样子，却像在驾一辆由隐形马匹牵引的轿车。

"离密涅瓦机关还有多远？"阿方索问。他把随身枪压在膝盖上，枪管还没冷。

"四十里路。"瓦莲京娜说，"你们这位老板会不会晕车？他从上车开始就没说过一句话。"

"他不是晕车。"唐璜说，"他是在想，公主殿下到底是从哪里得到的消息，能这么巧地把车停在那条巷子口。"

瓦莲京娜笑了一下，没回答。

西泽尔靠在车后排的真皮坐椅上，闭着眼睛。其实他没睡。他在听。听车轮压过路面的声音，听引擎的转速，听昆提良怀里艾莲微弱却均匀的呼吸——那小姑娘终于安静下来了，蜷在昆提良的军服外套里，像一只受伤的小兽。

也听瓦莲京娜的呼吸。

她的呼吸太平稳，平稳得不像一个刚刚从枪口下救出五个通缉犯的姑娘。

外交专车上不会有人开得这样自信——除非她预料到一场事故，或者打算亲手制造一场。

"停一下。"西泽尔忽然开口。

瓦莲京娜偏过头："嗯？"

"停车。"

她踩了刹车。轮胎在湿漉漉的石子路上拖出一道短短的痕迹。礼车停在橄榄林边上，引擎还在低低地震动，像一头蹲伏的兽。

"怎么了老板？"唐璜警觉地看向四周。

西泽尔睁开眼睛，没有看任何人，只是把目光投向车头那杆叶尼塞的国旗。旗子在风里抖着，旗杆下方接着一个不大的铜质底座，铜座边缘有一圈极细的、几乎看不见的接缝。

"把那个铜座拆下来。"他说。

阿方索愣了一下，翻身爬到车头，掏出随身的小刀，撬那个铜座。铜座很沉，接缝是新的，能看出最近被人打开过又重新合拢。撬了几下，铜座弹开，里面静静地躺着一个东西。

一个黑色的小铁匣。

只有半个拳头那么大，棱角极方正，表面有一层细细的霜，正在夜风里慢慢化成水珠。

阿方索的手指顿了一下，没敢碰。

"这是什么？"昆提良凑过来。

"红水银冷凝匣。"唐璜的声音忽然就低了下去，"底舱里见过的那种……盛活体神经的东西。"

车里一下子安静了。

瓦莲京娜的手还搭在方向盘上，金色的发带被风扯得猎猎作响。她没有转头，也没有说话，只是那双蓝色的眼睛慢慢地、慢慢地，从后视镜里看向西泽尔。

"殿下，"西泽尔的声音很轻，"您的车上为什么会有这个？"

瓦莲京娜笑了一下。那笑容这一次不像冰河解冻，倒像冰面下忽然裂开了一条缝。

"小骑士，"她说，"我也不知道。"

她说的是真话。西泽尔看得出来。

"车是从大使馆开出来的？"

"是。"

"今晚谁碰过这辆车？"

瓦莲京娜想了想，蓝眼睛微微眯起："司机，门卫，还有……换班的卫兵。我下午让人把车擦过一遍。"

"擦车的是谁？"

"一个新来的小子。"瓦莲京娜的指尖在方向盘上敲了一下，"两周前调进来的，叶尼塞本土口音，履历干净得像刚下过雪的院子。"

"干净得像刚下过雪的院子。"西泽尔把这句话在嘴里慢慢嚼了一遍，然后伸出手，去捏那个黑铁匣。

铁匣很冷，冷得像一块从极北冻土里挖出来的石头。他的指尖刚一碰到匣子，铁匣表面忽然亮起了一圈极细的红色纹路，像是有人在匣子里点了一根火柴。

唐璜倒抽一口冷气："老板别碰——！"

红色的纹路只亮了一瞬，又熄灭了。

匣子还是那个匣子，安静地躺在西泽尔的掌心，重得不像它的体积该有的样子。

"它认得你。"唐璜咽了口唾沫，"老板，这东西认得你。"

西泽尔没有说话。他把铁匣放在膝盖上，用指腹一寸寸地摸过去。匣子的边缘有一道极细的凹槽，凹槽里嵌着一小段东西——他把它抠了出来，借着车头灯的光看了看。

那是一截头发。

苍白的、几乎是银色的、极细的一截头发。

唐璜凑过来看了一眼，脸色立刻就变了："这是……"

"嗯。"西泽尔说。

那不是他的头发——他的头发是黑的。

可这截头发的颜色，他在镜子里见过。那是某种血脉的烙印，是只属于某一支家族男性的标记。他母亲偶尔会摸着他的头发叹气，说他还好，他幸运，他没有继承那个颜色。

阿方索的眼神也变了。他在君士坦丁堡的贵族年鉴里见过那种颜色的头发，画在某位早已不在人世的公爵小像旁边。

"老板，"唐璜的声音几乎是从齿缝里挤出来的，"这是有人……专门留给你看的。"

西泽尔把那截头发卷起来，收进胸口的内袋。他的动作很慢，慢得像在做一件很重要的事。

"走吧。"他说。

瓦莲京娜没有立刻发动车子。她转过头来，金发垂在肩上，第一次用一种近乎严肃的眼神看着他："小骑士，你要不要把那匣子扔了？"

"不。"西泽尔说，"扔了它，下一个就会出现在别的地方。"

"它可能会爆。"

"它不会。"西泽尔淡淡地说，"它是给我看的，不是用来杀我的。要杀我，方才在巷子口就该用炸药，而不是用一个冷凝匣藏在车头底下，等我自己掀开。"

瓦莲京娜看了他一会儿，忽然笑了一下："你这小脑袋瓜子……比德隆爵士好玩多了。"

她重新发动车子。礼车在橄榄林之间穿行，引擎声低沉地震动着夜色。

车里又安静下来。昆提良抱着艾莲，没有再开玩笑。阿方索把随身枪重新压在膝盖上，眼睛盯着窗外掠过的橄榄树影。唐璜靠在西泽尔身边，压低了声音：

"老板，那截头发——"

"别问。"西泽尔说。

"……是。"

唐璜识趣地闭嘴了。但他偷偷瞥了一眼西泽尔放在膝盖上的那只黑色铁匣。匣子在车窗外掠过的路灯光里一明一灭，像一只闭着的眼睛。

唐璜忽然有种很不好的感觉。他认识西泽尔很久了，他知道这位老板在什么时候是真的平静，什么时候只是表面平静。

现在西泽尔的指节，正死死扣在膝盖的布料上，扣得发白。

礼车驶过一座小石桥，桥下河水黑得像墨。瓦莲京娜忽然开口：

"小骑士，叶尼塞和翡冷翠的大使馆已经被人盯了很多天了。盯梢的人不是教皇国的，也不是君士坦丁堡的。"

"是谁？"阿方索问。

"不知道。"瓦莲京娜说，"他们换班很勤，每六个小时一次，每次都从不同的巷子里冒出来，不留任何痕迹。我父亲派了三个最好的探子去查，回来的只有一个。那个人回来的时候舌头被人剪掉了，胸口压着一张纸。"

"纸上写了什么？"西泽尔问。

瓦莲京娜从大衣口袋里摸出一张折得方方正正的小纸条，递给后座。

纸条很轻，几乎没有重量。西泽尔展开它。

上面只有一行字，墨迹是新的，写字的人手很稳，一笔一划像刻出来的。

——把那个孩子还回来。

西泽尔盯着那行字看了很久。

车窗外，密涅瓦机关的轮廓已经在远处的黑色山脊上隐隐露出。那是一座由灰色花岗岩砌成的巨大建筑，圆顶上立着一尊持矛的女神铜像，铜像在云缝里时隐时现，矛尖指向天空，像在指着什么遥远的东西。

"殿下，"西泽尔把那张纸条折回去，放进胸口的内袋，和那截苍白的头发放在了一起，"这张纸条上的'孩子'，您觉得是指谁？"

瓦莲京娜从后视镜里看着他，那双蓝眼睛在路灯掠过的光影里忽明忽暗。

她笑了一下，没有回答。

她只是把车开得更快了。

礼车冲进密涅瓦机关那道高耸的铁门。铁门在他们身后缓缓合拢，发出一声沉闷的、近乎叹息的轰响。

西泽尔的膝盖上，那个黑色的小铁匣，又亮起了一圈极细的红色纹路。

这一次，纹路没有熄灭。

而西泽尔，第一次清晰地感到——大衣下那副他已经几周未曾穿过的龙鳞甲，正在变沉，变热，像一头蛰伏了很久的东西，被什么唤醒了。

【English】

Chapter 157 — The Black Box

The last sliver of moonlight was swallowed by cloud as the limousine pulled out of Florence. Beyond the city the road ran between unbroken stretches of olive groves, their leaves churning in the wind with a dry, rustling sound, like a crowd of people speaking in low voices.

Valentina drove fast, and with an uncanny steadiness. The car was a heavy machine, fed on coal and compressed steam, smelling of burnt graphite and hot oil — yet she handled it the way someone might handle a carriage drawn by invisible horses. Now and then she glanced into the rearview mirror at the boys behind her. The wind tore her long golden hair into something like a banner, and the faint scent of honeysuckle she carried curled into the night air and settled, lightly, on every one of them.

"How far to the Minerva Institute?" Alfonso asked. His sidearm lay across his knee. The barrel had not yet cooled.

"Forty li," Valentina said. "Does your master get carsick? He hasn't said a word since he stepped in."

"He doesn't get carsick," Don Juan said. "He's wondering exactly how Her Highness happened to have her car waiting at the mouth of that alley at just the right moment."

Valentina smiled faintly and did not answer.

Cezar leaned back into the leather of the rear seat, eyes closed. He wasn't asleep. He was listening. To the wheels grinding over wet stone. To the pitch of the engine. To Elena's small, steady breathing in Quintilian's arms — the girl had finally gone quiet, curled inside his military coat like a wounded little animal.

And to Valentina's breathing.

It was too even. Too even for a girl who, an hour ago, had pulled five wanted men out from under the muzzle of a gun.

You did not drive a diplomatic car with that kind of confidence unless you expected a crash, or unless you intended to cause one.

"Stop the car," Cezar said.

Valentina tilted her head. "Hm?"

"Stop."

She hit the brakes. The tires drew a short black smear across the wet gravel. The limousine came to rest at the edge of the olive grove, the engine still throbbing low beneath the hood, like a crouching beast.

"What is it, boss?" Don Juan said, already scanning the dark.

Cezar opened his eyes. He didn't look at any of them. His gaze went to the small Yenisei flag mounted on the hood. It was shivering in the wind, and beneath its staff sat a brass pedestal, no larger than a fist. Around the edge of the brass ran a hairline seam, almost invisible.

"Pry off that mount," he said.

Alfonso blinked, then climbed up onto the hood, drew a small knife, and went to work. The pedestal was heavy. The seam was new — you could see where it had recently been opened and closed again. After a few moments it popped free, and inside, lying quietly, was a thing.

A small black iron box.

Half the size of a fist, with sharp square corners, the surface coated in a fine layer of frost that was slowly melting into beads of water in the night wind.

Alfonso's fingers stopped just short of it. He did not dare touch it.

"What is it?" Quintilian leaned in.

"Red-mercury condensing case," Don Juan said, and his voice dropped. "The kind we saw down in the cargo holds. The kind they used for living nerve tissue."

The car went silent.

Valentina's hand still rested on the wheel. The wind pulled at her golden ribbon, snapping it. She did not turn. She did not speak. Only her blue eyes moved, slowly, slowly, finding Cezar in the rearview mirror.

"Your Highness," Cezar said softly, "why is this in your car?"

Valentina smiled — only a little. This time the smile was not like a thawing glacier. It was like a sudden fissure cracking open beneath the ice.

"Little knight," she said, "I don't know."

She was telling the truth. He could see it.

"The car came out of the embassy?"

"Yes."

"Who touched it tonight?"

She thought. Her eyes narrowed slightly. "The driver. The doorman. And the relief guards. I had it cleaned this afternoon."

"Who cleaned it?"

"A new boy." Her fingertip tapped once on the wheel. "Brought in two weeks ago. Native Yenisei accent. Record as clean as a courtyard after fresh snowfall."

"Clean as a courtyard after fresh snowfall," Cezar repeated, turning the words over in his mouth. Then he reached out and took the black iron box in his hand.

It was cold. Cold like a stone dug from the permafrost of the far north. The instant his fingertips brushed it, a circle of fine red lines flared to life on its surface, as though someone had struck a match inside.

Don Juan sucked in a breath. "Boss — don't —"

The red lines glowed for an instant, and died.

The box was a box again, lying quietly in Cezar's palm, heavier than its size had any right to be.

"It knows you," Don Juan said. He swallowed. "Boss. That thing knows you."

Cezar didn't answer. He set the box on his knee and ran his thumb over it, inch by inch. Along one edge ran a hairline groove, and something was wedged into it. He worked it out and held it up in the wash of the headlights.

A strand of hair.

Pale. Almost silver. Very fine. Very short.

Don Juan leaned in, and his face changed at once. "That's —"

"Yes," Cezar said.

It wasn't his. His hair was black.

But the color of this strand — he had seen it before, in a mirror. It was a mark carried in the blood, borne only by the men of a certain family. His mother used to stroke his hair sometimes and sigh, and say that he was lucky, that he had not inherited that color.

Alfonso's expression had shifted too. He had seen hair of that color in the noble registers of Constantinople, painted into the miniature portrait of a duke long dead.

"Boss," Don Juan said, the words forced through his teeth, "someone left this. For you. Specifically."

Cezar rolled the strand of hair up carefully and tucked it into the inner pocket over his chest. He did it slowly, as if performing some quiet, important rite.

"Drive," he said.

Valentina did not start the car at once. She turned, her golden hair falling over one shoulder, and for the first time looked at him with something almost grave. "Little knight. Do you want to throw the box away?"

"No," Cezar said. "If I throw it away, another will appear somewhere else."

"It might go off."

"It won't." His voice was flat. "It was meant for me to see, not to kill me. If they wanted me dead, they would have used dynamite at the mouth of the alley — not hidden a condensing case under the hood and waited for me to lift it out myself."

She watched him a moment. Then, suddenly, she laughed. "That little head of yours. Far more entertaining than Sir Derron's."

She started the engine. The limousine threaded its way between the olive trees, the low growl of the engine rolling out into the dark.

The car fell quiet again. Quintilian held Elena and made no more jokes. Alfonso laid his pistol back across his knee, eyes on the shadows of trees skimming past the window. Don Juan leaned closer to Cezar, lowered his voice.

"Boss. That strand of hair —"

"Don't ask."

"…Right."

Don Juan knew when to stop. But he stole a glance down at the black iron box resting on Cezar's knee. In the flickering streetlight slipping past the window, the box pulsed dim and bright, dim and bright, like a closed eye.

A bad feeling settled in him. He had known Cezar a long time. He knew when his master was truly calm, and when he was only calm on the surface.

Right now Cezar's knuckles were dug hard into the cloth over his knee. White from the pressure.

The limousine crossed a small stone bridge. The water below ran black as ink. Valentina spoke again, suddenly.

"Little knight. The embassies of Yenisei and Florence have both been watched for many days now. The men watching aren't from the Papal States. And they aren't from Constantinople."

"Then who?" Alfonso asked.

"I don't know." Her voice was level. "They rotate every six hours. Each time, out of a different alley. They leave no trace. My father sent three of his best agents after them. Only one came back. When he came back, his tongue had been cut out, and there was a slip of paper pinned to his chest."

"What did it say?" Cezar asked.

She drew a small slip of paper from her coat pocket, folded neatly into a square, and passed it back.

It was light. Almost weightless. Cezar unfolded it.

A single line of writing. The ink was fresh. The hand was steady — every stroke as if carved.

— *Return the child.*

Cezar stared at the line for a long time.

Beyond the window, the silhouette of the Minerva Institute had begun to rise from the dark ridge in the distance. A vast structure of gray granite, crowned with a great dome on which stood a bronze goddess holding a spear. The bronze appeared and disappeared between the running clouds, the spear's tip aimed at the sky, as if pointing toward something very far away.

"Your Highness," Cezar said, folding the paper and tucking it into his inner pocket, beside the pale strand of hair. "This 'child' on the note. Who do you think it means?"

Valentina watched him in the mirror. Her blue eyes brightened and dimmed in the passing wash of the lamps.

She smiled, and did not answer.

She only drove faster.

The limousine plunged through the high iron gates of the Minerva Institute. Behind them, the gates swung slowly shut and closed with a heavy, almost sighing thud.

On Cezar's knee, the small black iron box woke again, a fine ring of red lines surfacing across its skin.

This time, the lines did not go out.

And for the first time that night, Cezar felt it clearly — the dragon-scale beneath his coat, the suit he had not worn in weeks, beginning to grow heavy. Beginning to grow warm. As if something old, that had been sleeping a long time, had just been called by name.
